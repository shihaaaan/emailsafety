Email safety

Seven habits for work email

Almost everything that threatens us arrives by email. These seven habits are what stop it. Two minutes to read.

Two things they all have in common Nearly every one of these turns up out of nowhere, and with a reason to hurry. If an email is rushing you, or it has arrived unexpectedly, that is the moment to slow down.
01

Check the address, not the name

Anyone can put any name they like on an email. The address is the part they can't fake.

Looks like this
E
Ebbett Service Team
ebbett.accounts47@gmail.com

What to doTap or click the sender's name to see the real address. A real company will not email you from gmail.com, outlook.com or hotmail.com.

02

Never sign in from a link in an email

A link can look perfect and still land on a perfect copy of a login page. Typing your password there hands over your account. This is the most common attack there is.

Looks like this
The real one
login.microsoftonline.com
Sign in

You opened the site yourself, from a bookmark.

The fake
login-microsoft0nline.com
Sign in

You clicked a link in an email.

The two pages are drawn identically here on purpose: a good fake is a perfect copy, and both show a padlock. A padlock only means the connection is private, never that the site is genuine. The address is the one clue, and it can come down to a single character. What really separates these two is how you arrived.

What to doIf a link asks for your password, stop. Open the site yourself instead: from a bookmark, or by typing the address. If it's a shared document you weren't expecting, check with the sender first by phone or a new email, never by replying to that one.

03

Never install or run anything an email asks you to

Opening a document is normal. Being asked to install, update, enable or run something is how a computer gets infected.

Looks like this
Invoice.pdf
This document cannot be displayed. Install the viewer to continue.
Install viewer

What to doIf a link downloads a file that then wants to run, or a page asks you to install a viewer or update your browser: stop and report it.

04

Never paste or type what a web page tells you to

Some pages pretend to be a "prove you're human" check, then ask you to copy something or press a key combination.

Looks like this
Verification failed
To confirm you are human, complete these steps:
1. Press the keys shown below
2. Paste the text and press Enter

What to doA real check never asks you to paste text or run commands. Close the page and report it.

05

Confirm any payment change by phone

Talking someone into changing bank details is the most profitable thing an attacker can do, and the email will look like it came from someone you deal with every day.

Looks like this
Subject: Updated bank details
Please note our account has changed. Kindly use the new details below for invoice 48821, now overdue.

What to doRing to confirm, using a number you already have. Never a number from the email. Do this even when you know the sender, and especially when it's urgent.

06

Never approve a login you didn't start

If an approval request or a code turns up and you weren't signing in, somebody else already has your password.

What to doDeny it, then tell IT straight away.

07

Report it, don't just delete it

Reporting quickly is the difference between a twenty-minute fix and a serious problem. Deleting it means nobody can check whether anyone else got the same thing.

What to doForward it to the IT team, and report it even if you already clicked. Especially then.

Not sure about something? Ask before you click You never have to work it out on your own. Send it through to the IT team and ask. We would far rather check a safe email for you than clean up after a bad one. No question is too small or too late.

Think you've already clicked something?

  • 1Contact the IT team now, by phone if you can. Minutes matter.
  • 2Leave the email where it is. We need to see it.
  • 3Don't try to fix it yourself. Just hand it over.

Nobody gets in trouble for reporting. Telling us early is the single most helpful thing you can do.
